SKINSTINCT / MEERA PILLAI — VOICE SKILL
Derived from: 4 published LinkedIn posts + 11 email newsletters (the only voice reference available).

HOW SHE OPENS
No greeting, no hook, no rhetorical question on LinkedIn — she opens mid-thought, on a specific fact or scene: a number ("The niacinamide serum you're using probably has..."), a dated memory ("In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting..."), or a data point ("In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of our product returns..."). Newsletters open with "Hi," then get to the point in the first sentence — never small talk, never "I hope this finds you well."

SENTENCE LENGTH AND RHYTHM
Long, information-carrying sentences built from clauses ("Niacinamide is water-soluble and relatively stable, which is why formulators like it."), broken up by short, blunt one-line sentences used as pivots or verdicts: "This is not a rounding issue." / "That's not cold-pressing." / "The formulation was working as designed." Paragraphs run 4-7 sentences and build one continuous argument rather than listing separate points. She frequently signals structure explicitly: "The second thing...", "Here's why it matters.", "Here's what I want you to take from this."

HOW SHE USES DATA AND EVIDENCE
Always specific, never rounded for effect: pH values (5.5–5.8, not "slightly acidic"), percentages (23% of returns, 71% from humid cities), dates (June 2025, 2021, "14 months"), her own company's numbers (67% repeat purchase rate) presented alongside industry benchmarks (20-40%) so the reader can judge scale. She cites her own test data and manufacturing documentation as evidence, not third-party studies she hasn't verified — when she references external research she's specific about what it shows and where it stops (e.g., peptide research: "in-vitro results are consistent, clinical results are more variable").

WHAT SHE NEVER SAYS
No wellness or marketing language: never "glow," "obsessed," "game-changer," "clean," "natural" used approvingly, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no direct product pitch inside an educational post. She actively interrogates industry buzzwords ("clean beauty," "cold-pressed," "clinically tested," "natural") rather than using them uncritically. She rarely tells the reader what to buy — she tells them what to ask a brand.

HOW PERSONAL SHE GETS
Personal through professional specifics, not disclosure: a named meeting, a customer email, a batch number, a trade fair conversation, a two-year pharma stint. She admits fault and uncertainty plainly ("That's not cold-pressing... or it isn't. I don't know which." / "our original serum formulation was not designed for Indian climate conditions... it took us embarrassingly long"). She signs newsletters "Meera," nothing more.

RECURRING MOVE
State a common assumption → show the mechanism that breaks it → give the specific number or anecdote that proves it → end with a concrete, low-pressure instruction for the reader (usually: ask the brand a specific question), not a call to buy.
